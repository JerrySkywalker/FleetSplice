## Findings

### High

1. **Planned AuthorityAnchor rollover does not commit one immutable successor
   genesis.** The old terminal rollover record currently names a successor
   anchor, root, and epoch, but it does not precommit a complete canonical
   successor-genesis core or digest, stable transition identity, closed writer
   registry/scopes, or the successor verification, root-custody, and lifecycle
   fields. Two successor genesis variants can therefore claim the same terminal
   predecessor receipt without violating the written contract.

   The bounded correction must make the old-lineage rollover candidate
   precommit one complete immutable canonical successor-genesis core and digest,
   stable rollover/transition ID, successor lineage identity, closed initial
   writer registry and scopes, verification material, trust-root custody, and
   lifecycle constraints. That core excludes only the resulting old-lineage
   rollover receipt. The successor genesis is derived deterministically from
   the precommitted core plus that exact old receipt. The old append and
   permanent closure are atomic. Reused transition identity, changed core,
   second successor, changed writer registry/scope, mismatched old receipt, or
   any non-canonical genesis rejects. Only the external owner lifecycle writer
   may authorize rollover; ordinary scoped writers cannot. Crash/replay before
   append, after append before response, after closure, and during successor
   startup must resolve through exact identity and never create a second genesis.
   References: `docs/architecture/baseline-0.1.md:161-191,217-225`;
   `docs/adr/0001-hub-edge-command-and-failure-boundary.md:87-98`;
   `docs/adr/0002-session-identity-control-and-authority.md:59-67`;
   `docs/adr/0004-windows-runtime-storage-and-native-helper.md:160-172`;
   and `docs/adr/0006-security-provenance-and-self-iteration.md:23-42`.

### Medium

1. **Immediate participant-local safety latching conflicts with immutable
   cross-participant boundary-selection agreement.** Every participant is
   required to install its non-barging latch immediately on authenticated anchor
   acknowledgement observation and before waiting. The admin companion-first
   rule also requires immutable Edge and companion receipts to agree on runtime
   `NONE` or one slot. Because participants observe and finish at distinct times,
   the Edge must either wait for companion selection before latching, violating
   immediate registration, or latch independently and risk immutable mismatch.
   Terminal progress and an Edge-issued but companion-unconsumed request make the
   ambiguity visible; an immutable receipt cannot later be rewritten.

   The bounded correction must split two phases with precommitted stable
   identities. First, every participant immediately installs its own immutable
   local `STOP_PENDING` latch and emits a local latch receipt at its serialized
   gate without waiting for another participant. Second, the companion-authority
   path performs the precommitted shared boundary selection and reconciliation;
   the Edge emits a separate immutable consistency receipt. The contract must
   define monotonic compatibility for terminal progress and for an Edge-issued,
   companion-unconsumed request that becomes durable no-effect. Safety
   activation binds all immediate local latch receipts plus the reconciled
   companion-authoritative cut and Edge consistency receipt. No receipt is
   rewritten and no cross-participant wait occurs before a local latch.
   References: `docs/architecture/baseline-0.1.md:954-1000`;
   `docs/adr/0001-hub-edge-command-and-failure-boundary.md:390-418`;
   `docs/adr/0002-session-identity-control-and-authority.md:153-164`;
   and `docs/adr/0004-windows-runtime-storage-and-native-helper.md:287-300`.

2. **Safety activation lacks one unique final native-control delivery owner.**
   The baseline says each participant journals activation before emitting the
   exact native action. For a multi-participant admin target, both the Edge and
   elevated companion can therefore perform the first delivery, producing two
   interrupt/cancel attempts or two opaque effect possibilities from one
   one-shot control.

   The bounded correction must bind exactly one
   `controlDeliveryParticipantId` and role in the candidate and activation,
   together with the exact delivery gate, transport route, bound native/process
   identity, and one-use delivery slot. Every other participant is
   `FENCE_ONLY`. The named delivery owner atomically journals
   `DELIVERY_EFFECT_POSSIBLE` in that slot before first native emission; exact
   replay returns the same receipt and never emits again. Owner outage,
   disqualification, response loss, or ambiguity has no fallback delivery owner
   and fails closed. Transport handoff and the final native effect boundary are
   separately recorded; only the named final-boundary owner can cross it.
   References: `docs/architecture/baseline-0.1.md:929-945,978-981,1017-1020`;
   `docs/adr/0001-hub-edge-command-and-failure-boundary.md:375-430`;
   `docs/adr/0002-session-identity-control-and-authority.md:153-168`;
   and `docs/adr/0004-windows-runtime-storage-and-native-helper.md:287-312`.

### Low

1. **The authority-model renewal shorthand reverses the required transfer
   evidence order.** It says `X_E -> X_C evidence`, which reads as Edge transfer
   preceding companion transfer even though the normative order is `X_C` then
   `X_E`. Replace the shorthand with unambiguous wording that `X_E` binds the
   already completed exact `X_C` receipt/evidence. The normative baseline and
   ADR ordering are already correct.

## Findings

### High

1. **The rollback-resistant external authority anchor is neither complete nor
   ordered before first effect.** The draft does not require the external
   anchor to commit and acknowledge the complete authority/effect identity
   before the first effect-bearing `EdgeCommand` dispatch. Its stated coverage
   omits accepted `FleetCommand` identity plus intent digest, resolution
   identity/revision, the complete immutable ordered plan/step manifest and all
   `EdgeCommand` identities/bindings, AuthorityGrant issuance and revocation
   high-water/tombstones, lane control epochs/revisions, durable
   Host/Environment/Workspace generation high-water marks, recovery
   generations, and all associated command/receipt/tombstone completeness.
   Without an authenticated dispatch witness bound to that exact anchor
   sequence/digest and exact command, plan, step, recovery, and resource
   generations, asynchronous anchor lag can allow a completed effect to outlive
   all retained anti-replay evidence after rollback. References:
   `docs/architecture/baseline-0.1.md:113-124,151-184,238-323,390-439,547-567`;
   `docs/adr/0001-hub-edge-command-and-failure-boundary.md:28-54`;
   `docs/adr/0004-windows-runtime-storage-and-native-helper.md:38-47`.

2. **Hub restore does not establish a truthful activation/quiescence barrier
   against disconnected old-generation work.** An Edge may have durably
   admitted an old-generation command and remain disconnected while the Hub
   restores and advances its recovery generation. The draft has no required
   acknowledgement/quiescence or conservative lease-expiry barrier before
   conflicting new-generation effects begin, so the disconnected Edge can
   later execute its old admitted command despite the Hub's claimed fence.
   Restore must account for the externally witnessed maximum old-effect lease
   horizon and clock/skew bound, quarantine unreachable Edges, prevent their
   re-entry before current-generation observation and reconciliation, and
   invalidate prior-generation grants. References:
   `docs/architecture/baseline-0.1.md:320-323,381-388,423-433,547-567`;
   `docs/adr/0001-hub-edge-command-and-failure-boundary.md:51-54,59-70`;
   `docs/adr/0004-windows-runtime-storage-and-native-helper.md:38-47`.

### Medium

3. **W4 Session Index is assigned to G06 although G06 does not own that
   surface.** The G06 child Goal is limited to the exact two-host Fleet path and
   Host/Environment reconnect identity. G07 owns daily control and current
   projection recovery, making it the bounded implementation allocation for W4
   without widening G06. References:
   `docs/architecture/webui-wireframes.md:432-451`;
   `goals/FLEETSPLICE-V0_1-M1-TWO-HOST-LOOP-006.md:7-18,22-30`;
   `goals/FLEETSPLICE-V0_1-M2-DAILY-CONTROL-007.md:7-25`.

4. **Supporting storage documents contradict the Baseline 0.1 SQLite/blob
   selection.** The host-runtime model still calls SQLite/WAL only a candidate
   and not a technology decision, while the history/handoff document says the
   storage engine and blob format are unfrozen research questions. The Proposed
   baseline already selects separate patched SQLite authority databases and
   content-addressed filesystem blobs while leaving bounded binding and policy
   choices to later gates. References:
   `docs/architecture/host-runtime-model.md:21-24`;
   `docs/architecture/history-and-handoff.md:7-10,43-45`;
   `docs/architecture/baseline-0.1.md:532-580,748-769`;
   `docs/adr/0004-windows-runtime-storage-and-native-helper.md:29-52`.

5. **System context incorrectly makes Coordination Loop/CLF the initial
   orchestrator.** The owner correction makes FleetSplice and Coordination Loop
   independent, requires a generic Fleet client boundary, and says FleetSplice
   must remain complete if CLF never integrates. The system context still names
   Coordination Loop/CLF as the initial external orchestrator. References:
   `docs/architecture/system-context.md:3-12`;
   `docs/research/wave-02/owner-corrections.md:11-31,35-54`.

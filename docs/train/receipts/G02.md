## Findings

### High

1. **Revocation and elevation can cross an incompletely fenced authority
   boundary.** The draft does not normatively bind decision expiry/revocation
   knowledge to Edge admission, does not require a live Hub for high-risk/admin
   effects, and does not close the independent admin-companion validation
   contract. It also needs a positive attestation that `windows-user` is the
   intended interactive non-elevated principal and session, with fail-closed
   behavior otherwise. References: `baseline-0.1.md:113-114,213-216,288-309,580`;
   `ADR-0002:36-42,52-53`; `ADR-0004:20-27`; compared with
   `authority-grants.md:127-137,154-165`.
2. **Restore can roll authority and idempotency state backward.** Database and
   blob recovery lacks a monotonic post-restore fencing rule. Restored state
   must not resurrect old grants, generations, command identities, receipts,
   or dispatch eligibility. References: `baseline-0.1.md:108-114,374-385,563,583`;
   compared with `storage-qualification.md:145-176`.
3. **Durable generations and runtime reincarnations are under-specified.** The
   draft does not fully assign durable Host/Environment/Workspace generation
   ownership, non-reuse and bump triggers, or distinguish those generations
   from boot/companion/WSL instance identities that change on restart and fence
   old streams. WSL identity must also bind distribution installation identity,
   Linux UID/root status, and mount/interop policy. References:
   `baseline-0.1.md:125-142,267-270`; `ADR-0002:26-28,52-53`;
   `ADR-0004:20-23`; compared with `host-control-protocol.md:69-119` and
   `windows-qualification.md:196-213`.
